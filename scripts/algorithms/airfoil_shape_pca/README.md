# Airfoil Shape PCA

This script builds a linear shape space for NACA 4-digit airfoils by principal component analysis (PCA) of their surface coordinates and shows how few modes are needed to describe the family. It draws 200 random airfoils with maximum camber $m \in [0, 0.06]$, camber position $p \in [0.2, 0.6]$ and thickness $t \in [0.06, 0.18]$, samples them at common cosine-spaced chord stations, and plots the explained variance, the mean shape, $\pm 2\sigma$ variations along the first three principal components, and the reconstruction error of 100 airfoils that were not used to build the modes.

## Overview

- Generates 81 cosine-spaced stations $x/c \in [0, 1]$ shared by every airfoil.
- Evaluates the NACA 4-digit camber line and thickness distribution and stacks the upper and lower ordinates into one vector of length 162 per airfoil.
- Runs PCA on the 200 training vectors with `compute_pod` from `scripts/_numerics.py` (shapes as columns).
- Projects 100 independent test airfoils onto the leading $K$ modes for $K = 0, \dots, 10$ and records the mean and worst RMS ordinate error.
- Prints the cumulative explained variance and test error for $K = 1, \dots, 5$ and draws a six-panel figure.

## Mathematical Background

### NACA 4-digit sections

With $x$ the chord fraction, the half-thickness (open trailing edge) and the camber line are

$$
y_t(x) = 5t\left(0.2969 \sqrt{x} - 0.1260x - 0.3516x^2 + 0.2843x^3 - 0.1015x^4\right)
$$

```math
y_c(x) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{m}{p^2}\left(2px - x^2\right), & x < p, \\ \dfrac{m}{(1-p)^2}\left(1 - 2p + 2px - x^2\right), & x \geq p. \end{cases}
```

The standard construction offsets the thickness normal to the camber line. Here it is added vertically, $y_{u,l} = y_c \pm y_t$, so that every airfoil is sampled at the same $x$ stations. The difference is small for cambers of a few percent.

### Shape vectors and PCA

Each airfoil becomes $\mathbf{s}_i = [y_u(x_1), \dots, y_u(x_n), y_l(x_1), \dots, y_l(x_n)]^T$. With the mean $\bar{\mathbf{s}}$ and the centred matrix $X = [\mathbf{s}_1 - \bar{\mathbf{s}}, \dots, \mathbf{s}_N - \bar{\mathbf{s}}]$, the thin SVD $X = U\Sigma V^T$ gives orthonormal modes $\boldsymbol\phi_k$ (columns of $U$) and variances $\lambda_k = \sigma_k^2/(N-1)$. A shape is approximated with $K$ modes as

$$
\mathbf{s} \approx \bar{\mathbf{s}} + \sum_{k=1}^{K} a_k \boldsymbol\phi_k,
\qquad a_k = \boldsymbol\phi_k^T (\mathbf{s} - \bar{\mathbf{s}})
$$

and new shapes are generated by choosing $a_k$, for example $a_k = \pm 2\sqrt{\lambda_k}$.

Because $y_t$ is linear in $t$ and enters the two surfaces with opposite signs, all thickness variation lies along one vector $`[\,y_t/t;\, -y_t/t\,]`$. The camber line is linear in $m$ but nonlinear in $p$, so camber variation needs a few modes.

## Implementation

- `cosine_stations(n)` returns $x_j = \tfrac{1}{2}(1 - \cos(\pi j/(n-1)))$.
- `naca_thickness(x, t)`, `naca_camber(x, m, p)` and `airfoil_vector(x, m, p, t)` build one shape vector.
- `sample_parameters(n, rng)` draws uniform $(m, p, t)$ in `M_RANGE`, `P_RANGE` and `T_RANGE`.
- `build_shape_matrix(x, params)` stacks the vectors as columns for `compute_pod`.
- `reconstruct(pod, shapes, n_modes)` projects onto the leading modes; `reconstruction_errors` returns the mean and worst RMS error for each mode count.
- `make_figure` draws the six panels; `plot_shape` closes the outline from a shape vector.
- `SEED`, `N_STATIONS`, `N_TRAIN`, `N_TEST`, `N_SHOW_MODES` and `MAX_MODES` set the experiment.

## Usage

```bash
python main.py                          # show the figure
python main.py --no-show --output .     # save the figure as a PNG in the current directory
```

## Output

![PCA of NACA 4-digit airfoils: explained variance, mean shape, first three modes and reconstruction error](airfoil_shape_pca.png)

The first two modes carry 53.1% and 43.8% of the variance and the first three together 99.89%. PC1 is almost pure thickness (99.8% of its squared norm lies in the antisymmetric thickness direction), so moving along it thickens or thins the airfoil about the mean camber line. PC2 changes the amount of camber and PC3 moves the camber peak fore and aft. On the 100 unseen airfoils the mean RMS ordinate error falls from $1.5 \times 10^{-2}c$ with the mean shape alone to $2.1 \times 10^{-3}c$ with two modes, $4.4 \times 10^{-4}c$ with three and $4.2 \times 10^{-5}c$ with five. The worst test airfoil stays three to five times above the mean for $K \geq 2$. The vertical axis of the shape panels is stretched by a factor of two.
