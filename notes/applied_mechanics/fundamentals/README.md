# Fundamentals of Applied Mechanics

These notes set up the tools used in every other part of the applied-mechanics section: the idealized models and units of a mechanics calculation, Newton's laws, vector algebra with forces and positions, the moment of a force about a point and an axis, the reduction of any set of loads to a resultant force and couple, and the free-body diagram that turns a physical problem into equations. Read them in order before [Statics](../statics/README.md); the last section of [Force Systems and Resultants](./force_systems.md) shows how surface pressure and shear computed by CFD become the forces and moments that structural analysis starts from.

## Table of Contents

### 1. [Introduction to Applied Mechanics](./intro.md)

Scope of the subject, the particle, rigid-body, deformable-body and continuum models, SI units and dimensional homogeneity, Newton's laws and the momentum balances of a body, and a step-by-step problem-solving procedure with a worked beam example.

### 2. [Vector Analysis in Mechanics](./vector_analysis.md)

Components, unit vectors and direction cosines, forces along a line, dot and cross products, the moment as a cross product, triple products, rotation matrices for changing coordinate frames, and cylindrical unit vectors.

### 3. [Force Systems and Resultants](./force_systems.md)

Concurrent, coplanar, parallel and general force systems, equivalent force–couple systems, the single resultant and its line of action, the wrench, resultants of distributed line loads, and the integration of fluid pressure and wall shear into forces and moments.

### 4. [Moments and Couples](./moments_couples.md)

Moment of a force about a point in scalar and vector form, Varignon's theorem, moment about an axis as a scalar triple product, couples as free vectors, and moving a force by adding a couple.

### 5. [Free-Body Diagrams](./free_body_diagrams.md)

The procedure for isolating a body, reactions of 2D and 3D supports and connections, two-force and three-force members, static determinacy and improper constraints, internal forces by the method of sections, and worked beam, frame and truss-joint examples.
