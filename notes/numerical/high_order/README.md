# High-Order Methods

High-order methods reach a given accuracy on smooth problems with far fewer unknowns than second-order schemes. These notes cover spectral methods (Fourier and Chebyshev collocation, aliasing and de-aliasing), the spectral element method, and discontinuous Galerkin methods with numerical fluxes and limiters.

Algebraic against exponential convergence, FFT differentiation, aliasing and the 3/2 rule, the Gibbs phenomenon, Chebyshev collocation for non-periodic problems, spectral elements, and the discontinuous Galerkin method for linear advection with h- and p-refinement and limiting.
